# Glass Substrate Protocol (GSP)

**Author and Principal Architect:** Sierra N. Warren  
**Organization:** Sierra Warren Developments, LLC  
**Research home:** swd-rnd/GSP

The Glass Substrate Protocol is a structural reliability, safety, security, liability-oversight, and governing protocol for consequential AI and autonomous or machine-mediated systems.

GSP is designed around a core distinction:

> **Capability is not authority.**

A model, agent, robot, service, workflow, or machine may be technically capable of performing an action without possessing legitimate authority to perform it.

GSP places accountability into the execution architecture itself by binding consequential action to authority, policy, evidence quality, risk and consequence controls, exact action semantics, controlled execution, runtime supervision, outcome verification, and reconstructable evidence.

## Bricolage engineering method

The forward GSP architecture deliberately combines mechanisms from:

- runtime assurance;
- safety engineering;
- formal methods;
- control theory;
- zero-trust security;
- identity and delegated authorization;
- capability security;
- cryptographic evidence;
- fault containment;
- incident response;
- distributed systems;
- human factors;
- assurance cases; and
- liability reconstruction.

The purpose is not to rebrand those disciplines. The purpose is to make them cooperate around the accountability problems created when AI systems can act autonomously.

## Protected lineage

The original GSP remains foundational. Its substrate concept, pre-execution governance, structural traceability, state reconstruction, evidence preservation, refusal paths, human authority controls, non-bypass intent, and accountability orientation remain part of the protocol lineage.

Later engineering strengthens the mechanisms used to realize those principles.
