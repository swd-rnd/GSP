### Authorship

I am the originator and principal architect of the Glass Substrate Protocol (GSP). This record establishes the current technical boundary of GSP while preserving the architectural lineage of the original protocol.

The original GSP is not discarded or replaced. Its substrate concept, structural traceability, state reconstruction, pre-execution governance, evidence preservation, non-bypass intent, human authority controls, and accountability orientation remain foundational.

Later engineering strengthens the mechanisms used to realize those principles.

### Current scope

GSP is a structural reliability, safety, security, liability-oversight, and governing protocol for consequential AI and autonomous or machine-mediated systems.

Its central engineering problem is not simply whether a system is capable of producing an action. GSP distinguishes capability from legitimate authority and binds consequential execution to:

1. identifiable authority;
2. authenticated policy and constraints;
3. verified evidence and input quality;
4. pre-execution risk and consequence controls;
5. exact action semantics;
6. controlled execution;
7. runtime supervision;
8. durable evidence;
9. observed outcome;
10. reconciliation; and
11. accountability and liability reconstruction.

AI is a principal use domain, but GSP is not defined by one model, vendor, application, or deployment stack.

### Category boundary

GSP is not merely:

- an AI application;
- an AI model;
- a policy dashboard;
- a post-hoc audit log;
- a universal policy database;
- a centralized learning service;
- a vendor-specific integration layer;
- a certification mark;
- a standards organization;
- G-BALO; or
- Sierra Certified™.

G-BALO and Sierra Certified™ remain historically and institutionally related work, but they do not define the technical ceiling or identity of GSP.

### Governing proposition

A consequential machine-mediated action should not be considered adequately governed merely because it was logged after execution.

GSP is designed so that a system can establish, before and during execution, whether the action is legitimate under the authority, evidence, policy, quality, risk, and system state that actually apply, and can later establish what happened with enough fidelity to reconstruct responsibility.

### Protected architectural lineage

The following are protected GSP lineage:

- governance embedded in the execution path;
- machine-verifiable evidence;
- reconstructable state transitions;
- durable and tamper-evident records;
- explicit human and delegated authority;
- refusal and halt paths;
- traceable constraints;
- separation of claimed intent from actual execution;
- resistance to silent bypass; and
- accountability that survives later review.

### Research and novelty boundary

This repository distinguishes authorship from novelty claims.

No statement in this baseline is proof that every GSP mechanism is globally first-of-kind. Where related mechanisms exist in standards, academic literature, industry systems, or open protocols, GSP will identify and compose with them rather than erase prior work.

The differentiated contribution of GSP is evaluated at the architecture and system-composition level, with dated design records maintained in this repository.
